# Demo Report 03: Geopolitical Forecasting Expert Discovery

## 1. User Query

```text
Find experts in geopolitical risk forecasting, crowd forecasting, prediction markets, strategic foresight, or national-security risk who have public evidence of recent work, reports, talks, institutional profiles, or forecasting-related expertise.
```

## 2. Parsed Criteria

### Must Have

- Public expertise in geopolitical risk, strategic foresight, crowd forecasting, prediction markets, or national-security forecasting.
- Institutional profile, publication, report, platform page, or public content.
- Clear reason they are relevant to forecasting or geopolitical risk.

### Nice To Have

- Experience with intelligence, government, think tanks, forecasting platforms, or risk advisory.
- Recent public work or active organization.
- Contact path through institution, consulting page, or profile.

### Exclude

- Generic political commentators with no forecasting, risk, or foresight evidence.
- Market pundits with no geopolitical or methodology relevance.
- Anonymous accounts without verifiable background.

## 3. Search Strategy

Sources searched:

- Good Judgment and superforecasting-related pages.
- Strategic risk and foresight institution pages.
- Geopolitical risk expert profiles.
- Prediction market and forecasting platform pages.
- Academic and policy search results.

Search concepts:

- geopolitical risk forecasting expert
- crowd forecasting prediction markets expert
- strategic foresight national intelligence expert
- superforecasting geopolitical risk
- prediction markets geopolitical forecasting

## 5. Fit Summary

The strongest matches for a research or expert-interview workflow are:

1. Good Judgment / Superforecasters.
2. Erin Sikorsky.
3. Mathew J. Burrows.
4. Hypermind.

These provide a useful spread across methodology, applied geopolitical risk, institutional foresight, and forecasting platforms.

This report also demonstrates why SignalReach needs evidence-backed recommendations. A generic web search for "geopolitical expert" would surface many commentators, but this query requires evidence of forecasting, risk methodology, or strategic foresight.

## 6. Suggested Interview Angles

### For Good Judgment / Superforecasters

- How do you define forecast quality?
- What makes a geopolitical forecast actionable for businesses?
- How should a research team combine expert judgment, crowd forecasts, and prediction markets?

### For Erin Sikorsky

- How should teams connect climate-security signals to geopolitical-risk planning?
- What public indicators are useful for early warning?

### For Hypermind / GeoPredictions

- How do users interpret probabilities from crowd forecasting or prediction markets?
- What sources and signals make forecasts more trusted?

## 8. Risks And Verification Needed

- Some experts may be better reached through institutional contact forms rather than direct messages.
- Wikipedia pages should be treated as discovery sources, then verified with primary institutional profiles before outreach.
- Prediction-market startups may be product competitors or adjacent tools rather than interview subjects.
- For expert discovery, quality depends more on evidence relevance than contact volume.
